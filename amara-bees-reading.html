The busy world of bees

Bees are small insects that play a giant role in nature. Most bees live together in groups called colonies, and a single honeybee colony can hold tens of thousands of bees. Inside the colony, every bee has a job to do. The queen lays all the eggs, the male drones help her, and thousands of female worker bees do almost everything else. Workers clean the hive, guard the entrance, care for the young, and fly out to find food. As a bee travels from flower to flower, it collects a sweet liquid called nectar and a fine yellow powder called pollen. Back at the hive, the bees turn the nectar into honey, which they store as food for the cold winter months when flowers no longer bloom.

Bees do far more than make honey. When a bee lands on a flower, grains of pollen stick to its fuzzy body. The bee then carries that pollen to the next flower, a process called pollination. Pollination allows plants to make seeds and grow fruit, so without bees, many of the foods people eat would slowly disappear. Bees have also developed amazing ways to share information. When a worker discovers a good patch of flowers, it returns to the hive and performs a special movement known as the waggle dance. The pattern of the dance tells the other bees exactly which direction to fly and how far to travel. Sadly, bees today face serious threats from disease, loss of habitat, and harmful chemicals called pesticides. Protecting bees is one important way to protect the food we all depend on.

Words to watch: colony, drone, nectar, pollen, hive, honey, pollination, pesticide.

Reading level

The passage is written at roughly a grade-5 level: varied sentence length, a clear main-idea-and-details structure, and target vocabulary introduced with context clues so the meaning can be worked out from surrounding words.

Step 2 · comprehension answers

1-B main idea   2-C key detail   3-B vocabulary in context   4-B cause and effect   5-B key detail   6-C inference / main idea

Question 6 is the true inference — the passage never says "bees help make our food" in one neat sentence, so she has to connect pollination with the foods people eat. If she gets the recall questions but misses 6, that is the skill to coach next: pulling an idea together from across the whole text.

Step 3 · matching answers

colony → C   drone → F   nectar → D   pollen → A   pollination → E   pesticide → B

Watch nectar and pollen — they appear in the same sentence and are easy to swap. The clue is the describing words: nectar is a "sweet liquid," pollen is a "yellow powder." Point her to those exact words.

Step 4 · fill-in-the-blank answers

1. nectar   2. pollen   3. pollination   4. colony   5. drone   6. pesticide   7. hive   8. honey

Sentence 4 (ants living in a colony) is a deliberate transfer item — it uses "colony" outside of bees, which checks whether she understands the word's meaning rather than just its place in this passage.

Extension activities (project capstone)

  • Draw the inside of a hive and label the queen, a drone, and a worker bee.
  • Find out which fruit or vegetable in your kitchen depends on bees, and write one sentence about it.
  • Write her own sentence for two or three of the vocabulary words — the hardest level, with no word bank.
  • Discuss: "What is one thing your family could do to help protect bees?"
