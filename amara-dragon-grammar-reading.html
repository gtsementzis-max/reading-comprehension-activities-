Skarra and the Empty River

High above the village of Ardenmoor, in a smouldering hollow near the summit of the mountain, there lived a dragon named Skarra. Her home was an immense cavern whose walls glittered with veins of copper, and at its centre lay her hoard — a mound of coins, crowns, and stolen silver that no one had ever counted. Skarra was formidable: her wings stretched wider than the village square, and a single breath could scorch a barley field to ash. Yet what the villagers dreaded most was not her fire but her patience. She was endlessly vigilant, and in four hundred years not one thief had crept past her unnoticed.

One grey autumn morning, a girl named Elin climbed the treacherous path to the cavern mouth. She carried no sword and no shield — only a bucket. The river below the mountain had run dry, she explained, because the dragon's constant fire had cracked the stone that fed the spring, and by winter the whole village would have nothing to drink. Skarra listened without blinking. For a long while she refused, insisting that the troubles of humans were no concern of hers, but Elin did not flinch, and at last the old dragon relented. That night Skarra flew to the summit and, with three careful breaths, melted a channel through the ice so that the water ran again. She asked the village for nothing in return — though she did, quietly, keep the bucket.

Parts of speech in this story. A noun names a person, place, animal, or thing: dragon, village, cavern, bucket. A verb shows an action or a state of being: climbed, listened, melted, was. An article is one of only three small words — a, an, the — and it always sits in front of a noun to point it out: a bucket, an immense cavern, the old dragon. Try the whole test on one line: The dragon guarded a glittering hoard. Here The and a are articles, dragon and hoard are nouns, and guarded is the verb.
