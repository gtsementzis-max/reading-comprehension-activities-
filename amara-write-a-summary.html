What is a summary?

The big idea, in your own words.

A summary is a short retelling of something you read. It keeps only the most important ideas and leaves out the small details. A good summary is much shorter than the original, and it is written in your own words — not copied sentence by sentence.

Quick test: if a friend read only your summary, would they understand what the whole passage was about? If yes, you did it well.
!

Four steps to a great summary

Follow them in order.

  1. Find the topic. In a few words, what is the whole passage about?
  2. Find the main idea of each paragraph. Ask: what is the one most important thing this paragraph is telling me?
  3. Cross out the small stuff. Examples, extra facts, and little details do not belong in a summary.
  4. Write 3 to 4 sentences in your own words. Put the main ideas together, shortest and clearest way you can.

Watch me do it

A summary of the marine life passage.

Step 2 · main idea of each paragraph

Paragraph 1: The ocean has different layers, from the bright, warm surface to the cold, dark deep, and different animals live in each one.
Paragraph 2: Ocean animals survive using special features called adaptations, such as camouflage and making their own light.

Step 4 · my finished summary

The ocean is the largest habitat on Earth, and it has different layers from the sunny surface down to the dark deep. Different animals live in each layer. To survive, ocean animals use special features called adaptations. For example, some fish use camouflage to hide, and some deep-sea animals make their own light.

Notice what I left out: tuna, sharks, the octopus, and the anglerfish were all interesting examples, but a summary keeps only the big ideas — so most examples got crossed out. I also used my own words instead of copying whole sentences.

For grown-ups: how to coach this ▾

What a strong grade-5 summary looks like

Three to four sentences, the main idea of each paragraph, no opinions, almost no examples, and clearly her own wording. Length is a fast signal: if her summary is nearly as long as the passage, she is retelling, not summarizing.

The two skills to watch

  • Main idea vs. detail. The most common slip is keeping a fun detail (the waggle dance, the anglerfish) while missing the bigger point. Ask: "is that the main idea, or just one example of it?"
  • Own words. If a sentence matches the passage word-for-word, have her cover the text and say it out loud first, then write what she said.

A gentle way to give feedback

Start with what worked ("you caught the main idea of paragraph one"), then offer one thing to try ("can we say this in fewer words?"). One improvement at a time is plenty.

Her writing and her check-marks are saved on this device, so she can come back and revise. There is no automatic score here on purpose — summarizing is a judgement skill, and your read of her work matters more than a number.
